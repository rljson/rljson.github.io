## Why Rljson?

<CardGrid stagger>
  <Card
    title="Normalized"
    icon="document">
    Data is organized into redundancy-free tables that are joined on the client.
    Only what changed travels over the wire.
  </Card>
  <Card
    title="Deeply hashed"
    icon="seti:lock">
    Every object carries a content hash. Hashes are the primary keys, so
    datasets are immutable and trivially comparable.
  </Card>
  <Card
    title="Database-oriented"
    icon="seti:db">
    The format mirrors relational structures, so importing and exporting to
    SQLite, MSSQL, MongoDB or the browser's IndexedDB is straightforward.
  </Card>
  <Card
    title="Decentralized"
    icon="puzzle">
    Hashes are computed without a server, which makes Rljson a good fit for
    local-first and peer-to-peer applications.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>
